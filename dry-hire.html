Who hires it

Three situations, one package

Visiting mixers

You are flying in and would rather not fly your kit in with you. Wireless that is legal here, timecode that talks to whatever camera package has been booked, and someone local to call if something goes sideways at 6am.

Productions with crew

You have a sound recordist already and need the package locally rather than shipping and clearing your own. Common on shorter blocks where the freight and carnet cost more than the hire.

Overflow and second unit

You are already running a package and need more channels, a second bag, or a splinter unit for a day. Happens more often than anyone schedules for.

Why hire locally

The frequency problem nobody mentions until day one

This is the single most common reason productions end up hiring here rather than shipping their own wireless, and it is almost always discovered too late.

Permitted bands for wireless microphones are set country by country. Singapore's are set by IMDA. Malaysia, Indonesia, Thailand and Vietnam each have their own, and none of them line up neatly with the United States, the United Kingdom or Australia. A transmitter that is entirely legal in Los Angeles can be illegal to switch on in the country you have just landed in, and the fact that nobody is likely to check does not make it a good position for a production to be in.

Beyond legality there is the practical problem. Singapore in particular is dense, vertical and spectrally busy. A channel count that behaves perfectly in a quiet location will start dropping out in the CBD or in a hotel ballroom. Coordination here is real work rather than a formality.

Hiring the package locally removes both problems at once. The kit is already legal where you are, and it has already been coordinated in these buildings.

Before you ship anything

Send me the model numbers and the frequency blocks of what you were planning to bring. I will tell you in one message whether it is legal in your shoot country. It is a two minute conversation that has saved productions an entire day.

Common questions

What does dry hire actually mean?

Dry hire is the equipment without an operator. You or your sound recordist run it yourselves. Wet hire means the kit comes with the person who owns it, which in this case means me. Both are available, and which one is right usually comes down to whether you already have a sound crew.

Do you hire outside Singapore?

Regularly. The package is airline-checkable and travels across the region constantly. Cross-border movement needs a carnet or a temporary import arrangement depending on the country, which has to be organised in advance. For work outside Singapore it is often simpler and cheaper for me to travel with the kit than to ship it separately, so ask about that option before assuming freight.

Can I hire just the wireless?

Yes. Partial hires are common, and channels are the thing people most often run short of. Tell me how many you need and for how long.

What does it cost?

It depends on duration, channel count and whether the hire stays in Singapore or travels. Weekly hires are priced below the daily equivalent. Send the dates and the channel count and you will have a figure the same day rather than a rate card that is wrong for your job.
