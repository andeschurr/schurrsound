Next: a motorbike chase

I have not recorded a bike chase yet, so this is the plan I would bring to a director, not a credit.

The rider wears a small microphone low in the collar or inside the helmet padding, covered against wind. It feeds a radio transmitter and a pocket recorder on the rider, because radio range breaks on a chase and a recorder on the body does not. I travel in the tracking vehicle with the antennas up high. The engine, the tyres and the passes are recorded on their own, so the edit can set the dialogue against the bike. If the helmets are closed, we agree before the day which lines get recorded again that evening, on location, while the actors still have the scene in them.

On an island for Beta Squad

Last to Leave the Island, on the Beta Squad channel: one hour and seven minutes, and more than 7 million views by November 2026. I recorded all the location audio. My audio assistant, Reza Feryanto, was incredible: he helped mic up the cast and supported me across every part of the job. It was shot in August 2025 on a remote island off Serang in Banten, Indonesia, for Baliprod Films.

Had the chance to work with Ande on a recent remote island shoot in Indonesia, and honestly he was an absolute weapon when it came to solving problems under pressure.

We had a pretty complicated multi-cam and PTZ setup with celebrity talent in a very challenging location, and Ande always found a way to make things work. One moment that really stood out was figuring out how to route audio across the setup by using the fibre optic line the PTZ team had already laid underwater for camera. Simple idea once you hear it, but it took quick thinking, solid technical knowledge, and great collaboration across departments to pull off smoothly.

More than anything, Ande just brings a calm, solutions-focused energy to set that makes a huge difference when things get hectic, and manages to do it always with a smile on his face! Really great to have in your corner on a production like this.
