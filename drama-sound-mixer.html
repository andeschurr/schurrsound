The department

Sound is not a solo job on anything larger than a documentary. I work as head of department with boom operators and sound utilities I have built long relationships with, particularly across Indonesia and Malaysia, and a great deal of what ends up on the track is theirs.

In Malaysia and Indonesia I source crew locally by preference: Malaysian boom and utility on Malaysian rates, which also helps a local crew quota. As a New Zealand citizen I work as a local hire in New Zealand and Australia: I pay my own travel and accommodation, and the kit travels with me as a full drama package.

Package

Sound Devices 833 with SL-2 expander, twelve Wisycom radio channels, DPA lavaliers, Sanken and Sennheiser booms, timecode to every camera, IFB to director and script, and comms. Airline-checkable.

Cover

Insured worldwide, equipment and liability, with certificates for production insurance requirements. Paperwork ready the day the deal memo lands.

For directors

With actors

It is not my job to tell a director how to shoot a scene. The narrative, the performance and the visuals all matter more than my track does. But directors usually have several ways a scene could go, so I speak up quickly, between setups, and offer rather than demand.

  • Miking up in the right sequence alongside costume and makeup, and rebuilding the rigs as often as the costume changes.
  • Watching the blocking, and being in the director’s or the DOP’s ear when it is needed and nowhere near it when it is not.
  • When a line can be saved in the field, we run with it, and never at the expense of the director’s vision or the performance.
  • Young casts are familiar ground: Lord of the Flies, Hunt for the Wilderpeople and Mr Midnight. A chaperone is present, I explain before touching, and the kids thread the lav under their clothing where they can.

For post

Working with post

Post can only optimise what the actor gave you on the day. The job on set is to hand it something worth stretching.

  • Before the shoot: sync, naming, track layout, report format and one contact, agreed with your post team.
  • On the day: everyone who speaks in the mix and on their own track, tracks named by person, one track map for every recorder, and dual recording throughout.
  • Timecode: one master, jammed to every camera and recorder at call and after lunch.
  • The world, on its own tracks: room tone and wild tracks at every location, and full surround libraries where the location deserves them.
  • At wrap: one merged sound report for all recorders, in the audio folder before the DIT leaves.
