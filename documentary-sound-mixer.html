Documentary

Documentary sound, small crews, real locations.

One camera, a schedule that moves, and a room nobody planned to record in. That is most documentary days. It is also the work I like best, which is lucky.

Documentary is most of what I do, and it is the work I am happiest doing. Small crews, real locations, no second take.

A documentary day is not a drama day. There is usually one camera, sometimes two. The schedule moves. The location is whatever the story is in, which means a kitchen with a fridge, a factory floor, a street, a boat. Nobody is going to turn the air conditioning off for you, and there is no time to light a room twice.

What that asks of a sound recordist is judgement rather than kit. Knowing which battles are worth having, when to say something and when to stay out of the way, and how to get a usable track out of a room that was never meant to be recorded in.

What a day usually looks like

  • Radio mics on your contributors, rigged so they can forget they are wearing them
  • A boom when there is someone to hold it, or a second perspective when there is not
  • Clean, separate tracks, labelled properly, so the edit is not a puzzle
  • Room tone and wild tracks at every location, because the edit will need them
  • Timecode if you are running multi-camera, or a plan that works without it

Working with small crews

I have done a lot of one-camera, one-recordist days: interviews, verite, follow-along. If the budget stretches to a boom operator the track will be better, and I will say so. If it does not, I will work the way that gets you the best result on your own.

I also do not need looking after. I will find my own way to location, sort my own kit, and add nothing to the pile of things you are already carrying. On a small crew that is worth as much as anything in the bag.

If the budget is tight

Say so early and say what it is. I would much rather be out on a job being useful than sitting at home waiting for a bigger one that may never call. Tell me the days, the format and what you have. If I can do it well at that number I will, and if I cannot I will say so without making it weird.

Where I work

Based in Singapore, one to three hours from every major production centre in Southeast Asia, with full working rights in Australia and New Zealand. Recent documentary work has taken the kit to Malaysia, Indonesia, Vietnam, Thailand and New Zealand.

Past documentary includes series for the BBC, Netflix and Animal Planet, and factual work for CNA and Mediacorp in Singapore. The full list is here.

Just ask

Dates, location, format, and what you have to work with. No pitch required and no hard feelings either way. If I am not the right fit I will say so and point you at someone who is.
