The Stages

The studios of Asia-Pacific, walked rather than googled.

Every studio in the region has a brochure. None of them tell you how long it actually takes to get there on a Tuesday morning, how deep the local crew pool really is, or what the place does not have. This series does.

I visit where I can, and I say plainly when I have not. Each write-up carries the same things: stage sizes and heights, water capability, what post exists on site, who the rental partners are, a door to door travel time I have measured myself, and an honest list of gaps. The gaps are the point. A facility list without them is a brochure with a different typeface.

Every studio in the series also sits on the production map as a second layer you can switch on. Filled markers are places I have stood in. Hollow markers are desk research, and the map says so.

A note on incentives

Rebate schemes change, and a wrong figure in a producer's budget is the one mistake that is never forgiven. Where I have not verified a scheme directly with the film commission, the map shows no figure at all rather than an approximate one.
