Shooting in Johor: Iskandar Malaysia Studios

Five stages, two big water tanks and a Dolby Atmos room, just over the Tuas crossing. I went up in August with an email sent the day before and no appointment.

First lesson, in the arrivals hall at the Second Link: my Singapore SIM would not call a Malaysian number. Someone on the checkpoint staff lent me their phone. The studio line rang out, twice. So I just turned up. Four of them came out and gave me two hours.

Getting there

It is the Second Link at Tuas, not the Causeway. The Causeway is 30 km east, and it is the one your driver will assume.

  • Grab cannot drop off or pick up inside the checkpoint, so you cross on the CW7 bus.
  • By public transport it took me 3 hours out, first time, with plenty of standing about. Back was 1 hour 47. Under S$20 for the day, four Grabs included.
  • On the way back the bus lane goes up the outside of the car queue at the Malaysian exit. A production car sits in that queue. The bus doesn’t.
  • Quickest from Singapore: car or Grab to Tuas Link, CW7 across, Grab to the gate. About 2 hours.
  • I crossed on a public holiday and queued about five minutes each way. Don’t plan on that.
  • Not on a Singapore or Malaysian passport? Fill in the Malaysia Digital Arrival Card in the 3 days before you cross. Singapore PR doesn’t get you out of it.

What is there

  • Five stages, about 100,000 sq ft between them: two at 15,000, two at 20,000, and Stage 5 at 30,000 with the water tank.
  • Water is the reason to come. The paddock tank is 65 m square and 1.4 m deep, billed as the biggest in Southeast Asia. The deep tank is 18 m across and 5.6 m down. A ship hull already sits in its own water on the back lot.
  • Standing sets: a colonial street, a hospital, a police station. The hospital is right by the production offices for stages 3 and 4, which you’ll appreciate pushing a sound cart in the heat.
  • Two TV studios. One is now a virtual production stage, run with Oceanus Media Global. The other is a 700-seat audience studio with not much on, so you can likely get it.
  • What surprised me most: post on site. A Dolby Atmos mix stage, ADR, Foley, grade and DCP. You can shoot and finish in the same country.
  • I expected a landlord with big empty rooms. It’s more of a campus: camera and lighting hire (True Color Media, One Cool Hang Wan Rental), Archipelago Special Effects, art and props, a medic, and Camou College, which trains crew.

Is anyone shooting there?

Yes. Pendekar (Warrior), with Iko Uwais, started filming at the studios in early November and is due to wrap in late December, Screen Daily reports. The public credit list stops around 2019, so the place looks quieter than it is.

Crew

Most Malaysian sound crew live in KL, four hours or more by road, so on a Johor job you pay for their beds as well as their days. From Singapore it is an hour's drive and a border. No flights.

Claiming FIMI, Malaysia's rebate? It is 30%, up to 35% with the cultural test, and was extended in March for another five years. It also needs a minimum share of Malaysian crew: 30% in the guidelines, and FINAS said in 2025 it would rise to 40%. Malaysian crew count towards it. Singapore crew don’t. Put it in the crew plan on day one and check the current rules with FINAS.

Sound

Two hours on a lot isn’t an acoustic test. That takes a working day, a recorder and nobody else on the stage. But inside, the stages were really quiet. I didn’t hear a thing. Even with the elephant doors open you get that slight pressure of a properly built box, not a shed.

Outside is another story. I heard planes from Singapore, air force ones too. Plan for them on the back lot and anything you shoot outside.

What I could not check

  • The LED wall size. It is not published.
  • Door to door from Changi. That is from map data, not driven.
  • What is standing on the back lot this month. It changes.

Who to call

Iskandar Malaysia Studios, 1 Persiaran Layar Perak, 79250 Iskandar Puteri, Johor. +607 560 1888.

They gave a stranger with a borrowed phone two hours and straight answers, some of them to awkward questions. Not every studio does that.
