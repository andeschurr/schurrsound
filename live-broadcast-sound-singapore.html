You bring the broadcast. I am the sound on the ground.

A live cross has no second take and no fix in post. Your presenter needs to hear the gallery, the gallery needs to hear the presenter, and it has to arrive at the level your broadcaster asks for, first time.

I am a production sound mixer based in Singapore. I turn up with radio mics and IFB that are legal on local frequencies, plug into your camera or your encoder, and stay on it until you are off air.

  • IFBYour director or gallery in the presenter’s ear, and in mine.
  • Audio to your broadcasterA clean mix into your camera or your encoder, a LiveU or similar, at the level your broadcaster asks for. More than one broadcaster, more than one feed.
  • Phone and laptop crossesWhen the cross goes out through a phone or a laptop, it still gets a proper microphone.

Flying a broadcast in?

Crews come into Singapore from the UK, Australia, New Zealand and further for sport, news and events. Bringing radio mics across borders means carnets, excess baggage and frequencies that may not be legal here. A sound mixer who is already here takes all three off your list.

You get someone who lives here, knows which frequencies are legal, and does not mind a two in the morning call.

Before you enquire

What is a live cross?

A live cross is when a studio programme cuts to a presenter or reporter on location, live. The crew on location sends picture and sound back to the studio, and the studio sends its talkback to the presenter’s earpiece.

What does the sound mixer do on a live cross?

Mics the presenter and any guests, mixes them, and sends that mix to the camera or the encoder at the level the broadcaster asks for. Gets the studio’s talkback (IFB) into the presenter’s ear. Keeps a spare microphone ready on its own frequency.

Sound recordist, sound mixer, audio operator: who do I ask for?

On a live cross they are the same person. In the UK and Australia the job is usually called sound recordist. My title is production sound mixer. Ask for whichever your call sheet uses.

Can you work with our camera crew and our broadcaster’s kit?

Yes. I work to your camera operator or producer, feed whatever you are sending with, and match your reference level and track layout.

Do you supply the microphones and the IFB?

Yes: handheld and lavalier radio mics and IFB, all on frequencies that are legal in Singapore. If your team prefers its own system I will run that instead.

Send me the dates and the location.

And what you are feeding: camera, encoder or phone. That is enough to know whether I am free and what to bring.
